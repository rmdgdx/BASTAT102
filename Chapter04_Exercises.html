1
A mall analyst reports: “Average shopper spend is ₱3,850 with a standard deviation of ₱2,900, from 480 shoppers. So the true average could plausibly be anywhere from about −₱1,834 to ₱9,534.”What is the fundamental error in this statement?
AThe analyst used the standard deviation of individual shoppers where the standard error of the mean belonged. The correct SE is 2900/√480 = ₱132.37, giving [₱3,590.56, ₱4,109.44].
BThe interval is correctly built but uses the wrong multiplier; 2.58 should replace 1.96.
CA sample of 480 is far too small to support any interval; at least 1,000 respondents are required before an interval may be quoted.
DThe analyst should have reported the median instead, because shopper spending is right-skewed.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. The analyst used the standard deviation of individual shoppers where the standard error of the mean belonged. The correct SE is 2900/√480 = ₱132.37, giving [₱3,590.56, ₱4,109.44].

The standard deviation s = ₱2,900 describes how much individual shoppers differ from one another. The interval, however, is a statement about the mean, and the mean varies far less than an individual does. The right quantity is the standard error: 2900/√480 = 2900/21.909 = ₱132.37. The analyst overstated the uncertainty by a factor of √480 ≈ 21.9. The give-away is the negative lower bound: no shopper can spend less than nothing, so an interval that runs below zero cannot be describing a plausible average.

2
Delivery times for a logistics firm have μ = 42 minutes and σ = 6 minutes. An auditor takes a random sample of 36 deliveries.What is the probability that the mean of those 36 deliveries exceeds 44 minutes?
A0.9772
B0.0228
C0.3694
DEssentially zero — below 0.0001
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. 0.0228

The question is about a mean, so the relevant spread is the standard error: SE = 6/√36 = 6/6 = 1.00. Then z = (44 − 42)/1.00 = 2.00, and the area beyond z = 2.00 is 0.0228. Compare this with the probability that a single delivery exceeds 44 minutes, which is 0.3694 — the mean of 36 is about 16 times less likely to stray that far, and that gap is the whole practical value of the CLT.

3
An analyst has 350 consecutive daily sales records for a café, listed in chronological order. Wanting a sample of 50, she computes k = 350/50 = 7, picks a random start of 3, and takes every 7th record thereafter.What is the specific danger in this design?
AThe interval k should have been computed as n/N rather than N/n.
BThe random start makes the design equivalent to convenience sampling.
CThe list has a weekly cycle and k = 7 matches it exactly, so every selected record falls on the same day of the week — the sample is systematically unrepresentative.
DSystematic sampling is a non-probability method and cannot support inference at all.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. The list has a weekly cycle and k = 7 matches it exactly, so every selected record falls on the same day of the week — the sample is systematically unrepresentative.

Systematic sampling is normally an excellent, cheap approximation to an SRS. Its one failure mode is periodicity: if k is a multiple of a cycle length in the frame, every selected unit occupies the same position in that cycle. Daily sales records have a strong weekly cycle, and k = 7 locks the sample onto a single weekday. The resulting mean estimates that weekday's average, not the week's. The fix is either to reorder the frame, or to choose a k that shares no common factor greater than 1 with the cycle length — that is, a k relatively prime to it. Against a 7-day cycle, k = 9 (n ≈ 39) is safe, while k = 14 would be the worst possible choice, since 14 is itself a multiple of 7.

4
A tenant renewal-intention survey contacted 1,320 eligible businesses and received 480 completed responses. The research manager writes: “480 respondents is a large sample, so our results are reliable. Next round we will contact three times as many businesses to be even safer.”What is the most serious problem with this reasoning?
AThe low response rate inflates the standard error, so applying the finite population correction would resolve it.
BThe manager should report the margin of error alongside the sample size, after which the design would be sound.
C480 is not a large sample by survey standards, so the manager's premise is simply false.
DThe response rate is 36.4%, so nearly two-thirds of the intended sample is missing. The risk is non-response bias, which does not shrink as n grows — contacting 3,000 at the same rate multiplies respondents without touching the bias.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. The response rate is 36.4%, so nearly two-thirds of the intended sample is missing. The risk is non-response bias, which does not shrink as n grows — contacting 3,000 at the same rate multiplies respondents without touching the bias.

A response rate of 480/1320 = 36.4% means the sample that exists is the self-selecting third who chose to reply. If satisfied tenants and dissatisfied tenants reply at different rates — and they almost always do — the sample mean is centred on the wrong number. Raising n improves precision, which is measured by σ/√n, but bias is a shift in the centre of the sampling distribution and appears in no formula in this chapter. A bigger biased sample produces a tighter interval around a wrong value: worse, not better, because it looks more authoritative.

5
A property group's 2,400 tenants divide into 1,450 retail, 560 food-and-beverage and 390 services businesses. A proportionally allocated stratified sample of n = 200 is required.How many tenants should be drawn from each stratum?
ARetail 145, F&B 56, Services 39
BRetail 121, F&B 47, Services 32
CRetail 67, F&B 67, Services 66
DRetail 100, F&B 60, Services 40
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. Retail 121, F&B 47, Services 32

Proportional allocation sets nh = n × (Nh/N). Retail: 200 × 1450/2400 = 120.83 → 121. Food-and-beverage: 200 × 560/2400 = 46.67 → 47. Services: 200 × 390/2400 = 32.5 → 32. The rounded parts sum to 200, which is the check you should always perform. Note that 32.5 sits exactly on the rounding boundary and is rounded down so the total lands on 200; when the parts do not sum to n, adjust whichever stratum's rounding was closest to the .5 boundary, and say which one you adjusted.

6
From a survey of 480 shoppers, an analyst isolates the 12 who described themselves as luxury buyers. Their spending is heavily right-skewed. She reports: “Mean luxury spend is ₱18,400, standard error 2900/√12 = ₱837, so we are 95% confident the true figure lies between ₱16,759 and ₱20,041.”Which criticism is the most damaging?
AThe interval should have used the t-distribution rather than 1.96.
BLuxury buyers are not a stratum, so no separate estimate may ever be reported for them.
CWith n = 12 drawn from a heavily skewed population, the CLT has not taken effect, so the sampling distribution of x̄ is not approximately normal and no z-based interval is defensible.
DThe standard deviation of the luxury subgroup will differ from ₱2,900, so the arithmetic uses the wrong σ.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. With n = 12 drawn from a heavily skewed population, the CLT has not taken effect, so the sampling distribution of x̄ is not approximately normal and no z-based interval is defensible.

The CLT delivers approximate normality for x̄ only when n is large enough relative to how skewed the population is. The n ≥ 30 guideline already assumes moderate skew; with heavy skew, 30 is not enough, and 12 is far from it. The sampling distribution here retains the population's right tail, so a symmetric interval around x̄ misstates both endpoints. The honest report is descriptive — “12 self-identified luxury buyers spent between X and Y, median Z” — with an explicit statement that the subgroup is too small for inference.

7
A tenant survey draws n = 400 from a population of N = 1,240 tenants. The sample proportion satisfied is p̂ = 0.68.What is the correctly computed standard error of the proportion?
A0.00117, because the SE must be divided by n.
B0.02833, after applying the correction factor.
C0.02332 — the finite population correction does not apply to proportions.
D0.01920, after applying the finite population correction.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. 0.01920, after applying the finite population correction.

First the uncorrected SE: √[0.68 × 0.32 / 400] = √0.000544 = 0.023324. Then check the sampling fraction: n/N = 400/1240 = 32.3%, comfortably above the 5% threshold, so the fpc applies. fpc = √[(1240 − 400)/(1240 − 1)] = √(840/1239) = 0.82339. The corrected SE is 0.023324 × 0.82339 = 0.019205. Skipping the correction would overstate the standard error by 21.4% and the margin of error would be ±4.57 percentage points instead of the correct ±3.76.

8
With σ ≈ ₱2,900 and n = 480, a survey's 95% margin of error on mean spend is ±₱259.44. The board asks for a margin of roughly ±₱130 next year and assumes the sample must roughly double.What sample size is actually required, and what does that reveal?
AAbout 1,920 — the SE falls with √n, so halving the margin requires quadrupling the sample. Precision is quadratically expensive.
BAbout 3,840 — halving the margin requires eight times the sample.
CAbout 960 — the margin is inversely proportional to n, so halving the margin doubles the sample.
DAbout 640 — the relationship is roughly linear over this range.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. About 1,920 — the SE falls with √n, so halving the margin requires quadrupling the sample. Precision is quadratically expensive.

The margin is 1.96σ/√n, so it is inversely proportional to √n, not to n. To halve it you must quadruple n: 480 × 4 = 1,920. Checking directly, 1.96 × 2900/√1920 = ₱129.72. This is the economic heart of the chapter — each additional unit of precision costs disproportionately more than the last, which is why national polls settle near n = 1,000 and why the right question is usually “is ±₱260 good enough for this decision?” rather than “how do we get to ±₱130?”

9
A retail group operates across four regions and must publish a national average visit-spend figure and a separate reliable figure for each region. Regional visit volumes are NCR 47.9%, Luzon outside NCR 28.7%, Visayas 14.2%, Mindanao 9.2%. Budget allows n = 1,200.Which design best meets both requirements?
AA cluster sample of 12 randomly chosen malls with 100 visits each, since it is the cheapest to field.
BA convenience sample of 300 per region, since equal counts make the regions comparable.
CA stratified sample with region as the stratifying variable, allocated proportionally to 575 / 345 / 170 / 110, with the Mindanao stratum topped up if its own margin proves too wide.
DA simple random sample of 1,200 visits drawn nationally, since an SRS is the statistical benchmark.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. A stratified sample with region as the stratifying variable, allocated proportionally to 575 / 345 / 170 / 110, with the Mindanao stratum topped up if its own margin proves too wide.

Two requirements point to stratification. First, guaranteed subgroup coverage: proportional allocation gives NCR 1200 × 0.479 ≈ 575, Luzon 344.4 → 345 after nudging the rounding so the four parts sum to 1,200, Visayas 170, Mindanao 110, so no region can come up empty by chance. Second, precision: because spending patterns differ between regions but are relatively homogeneous within them, stratifying removes the between-region variability from the sampling error, so the national estimate is more precise than an SRS of the same size. The note about topping up Mindanao is the practically important refinement — proportional allocation is the standard choice for the national estimate, but n = 110 may give that region a margin too wide to act on, and disproportionate allocation with weights applied afterwards is the standard remedy. (Neyman's optimal allocation is a different thing again — it sets nh ∝ Nhσh to minimise the variance of the national estimate.)

10
An intern reports a 95% interval for mean monthly spend of [−₱1,834, ₱9,534] and asks whether the negative lower bound is “just how confidence intervals work.”What does the negative bound actually tell you?
AIt is normal and should be reported as-is; intervals frequently extend beyond the range of the variable.
BIt means the distribution of spend is left-skewed and the mean is unreliable.
CIt indicates that the confidence level should be lowered from 95% to 90% until the bound becomes positive.
DIt is a symptom of a computational error — spend cannot be negative, so an interval for its mean that extends below zero must have been built on the wrong measure of spread.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. It is a symptom of a computational error — spend cannot be negative, so an interval for its mean that extends below zero must have been built on the wrong measure of spread.

An interval for a mean should sit comfortably inside the plausible range of the variable, and comfortably narrower than the range of individual values, because means vary less than individuals do. A lower bound below zero on a strictly non-negative quantity is a red flag that points at exactly one thing here: the standard deviation of individuals was used where the standard error of the mean belonged. Recomputing with SE = 2900/√480 = ₱132.37 gives [₱3,590.56, ₱4,109.44], which is both plausible and useful. Treat impossible endpoints as a diagnostic, not a curiosity.

11
Historically 15% of a bank's loan applications are declined. A branch audit samples 200 applications at random.What is the probability that more than 20% of the sampled applications were declined?
A0.0238
B0.9762
C0.4443
D0.0385
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. 0.0238

Check the condition first: np = 200(0.15) = 30 and n(1−p) = 200(0.85) = 170, both far above 5, so the sampling distribution of p̂ is approximately normal. Its standard error is √[0.15 × 0.85 / 200] = √0.00063750 = 0.025249. Then z = (0.20 − 0.15)/0.025249 = 1.98, and the area beyond is 0.0238. Practical reading: if this branch really is declining 20% of applications, that is a roughly 1-in-42 event under the historical rate — unusual enough to justify a closer look, though not by itself proof of a policy change.

12
A trading desk manager argues: “Our strategy has lost money nine days running. By the Law of Large Numbers, a run of gains is now overdue, so we should increase position size.”How should this argument be assessed?
AIt is sound if the daily returns are independent, since independence is what the Law requires.
BIt misuses the Law. The Law says the running average converges to the population mean as n grows, by diluting early results under new ones — it never predicts that individual future outcomes will compensate for past ones. This is the gambler's fallacy.
CIt is sound only if the nine losses were themselves a random sample.
DIt is unsound because the Law applies to proportions, not to monetary returns.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. It misuses the Law. The Law says the running average converges to the population mean as n grows, by diluting early results under new ones — it never predicts that individual future outcomes will compensate for past ones. This is the gambler's fallacy.

The Law of Large Numbers guarantees that x̄ → μ as n → ∞. It achieves this by swamping: after ten thousand more days, nine unusual days carry almost no weight in the average. Nothing in the Law makes any individual future day more likely to be a gain. Believing otherwise is the gambler's fallacy, and increasing position size on the strength of it converts a statistical misunderstanding into a leveraged loss. Worse, in a trading context the nine losses may be evidence that μ itself is negative — the very parameter the Law says the average will converge to.

13
A brand posts a poll to its Instagram story. 8,400 followers respond. The report states: “84% of customers prefer the new packaging (margin of error ±1.1 percentage points).”Which single objection most undermines the report?
AThe sample of 8,400 is too small relative to the customer base to justify a margin that narrow.
BThe margin of error should have been computed at 99% confidence for a claim this strong.
CThe finite population correction was omitted, so the true margin is wider than ±1.1.
DThe margin of error presupposes a probability sample. Respondents here self-selected from followers who happened to see the story, so no selection probability exists and the ±1.1 figure has no meaning, however large n is.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. The margin of error presupposes a probability sample. Respondents here self-selected from followers who happened to see the story, so no selection probability exists and the ±1.1 figure has no meaning, however large n is.

Every formula in this chapter carries the same standing precondition: a probability sample, in which each unit has a known non-zero chance of selection. An Instagram story poll satisfies none of it — reach is determined by an algorithm, followers are not customers, and responding is voluntary. What the poll measures is the preference of engaged followers who saw the story and chose to tap, a group with an obvious enthusiasm bias. The ±1.1 is arithmetically computable and substantively empty; quoting it lends a biased result the authority of precision.

14
Planning a follow-up survey with σ estimated at ₱2,900, a research manager wants a 95% margin of error of ±₱200 on mean spend.What is the required sample size?
A808
B569
C404
D28
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. 808

Set the margin equal to the target and solve: E = 1.96σ/√n, so n = (1.96σ/E)² = (1.96 × 2900 / 200)² = (28.42)² = 807.7 → round up to 808. Always round up: rounding down leaves the margin slightly wider than the target, which is the one direction you promised not to go. Verify: 1.96 × 2900/√808 = ₱199.96 ✓.

15
Three distributions are in play whenever you sample: the population, one particular sample, and the sampling distribution of x̄.Which statement correctly distinguishes them?
AThe population and the sampling distribution share a shape; only the sample differs, because it is finite.
BThe population has spread σ and an arbitrary shape; a single sample has spread s ≈ σ and mirrors the population's shape; the sampling distribution of x̄ has spread σ/√n and is approximately normal for large n.
COnly the sampling distribution has a mean equal to μ; the population mean is estimated by x̄.
DAll three have the same standard deviation σ; only their means differ.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. The population has spread σ and an arbitrary shape; a single sample has spread s ≈ σ and mirrors the population's shape; the sampling distribution of x̄ has spread σ/√n and is approximately normal for large n.

This three-way distinction is the conceptual spine of the chapter. A population of delivery times may be right-skewed with σ = 6. A single sample of 36 of them will also look right-skewed, with s near 6 — collecting more data does not make one sample's histogram narrower, only better resolved. The sampling distribution of x̄ is a different object entirely: it describes how the sample mean would vary across all possible samples of size 36, it is centred on μ, its spread is 6/√36 = 1, and the CLT makes it approximately normal even though the population is not. Only this third distribution narrows as n grows, and only this third one is guaranteed a shape.

16
Monthly electricity cost per branch has μ = ₱250,000 and σ = ₱40,000. A random sample of 100 branches is audited.What is the probability that the sample mean falls below ₱244,000?
A0.0013
B0.4404
C0.0668
D0.1587
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. 0.0668

SE = 40,000/√100 = 40,000/10 = ₱4,000. Then z = (244,000 − 250,000)/4,000 = −1.50, and the area below z = −1.50 is 0.0668. Reading it back to management: if the true average really is ₱250,000, an audit of 100 branches would show a mean this low about seven times in a hundred — noticeable but not yet remarkable.

17
A property group wants to survey all 1,240 of its tenant businesses. The only complete contact list available is the e-mail directory, which covers 1,015 tenants; the remaining 225 — mostly small stall operators — have no e-mail on file. The team proposes an online-only survey of the 1,015.How should the design be corrected, and why?
AExtend the frame to cover the missing 225 through a second contact mode — phone or in-person intercept at the stalls — because the gap between frame and population is coverage error, and the excluded group differs systematically from those on the list.
BProceed as proposed but increase the sample size, since a larger sample compensates for the missing tenants.
CProceed as proposed and note the limitation in a footnote; a 82% frame coverage is acceptable by industry convention.
DAbandon the survey, since no valid inference is possible when the frame is incomplete.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. Extend the frame to cover the missing 225 through a second contact mode — phone or in-person intercept at the stalls — because the gap between frame and population is coverage error, and the excluded group differs systematically from those on the list.

The 225 missing tenants are not a random subset: they are disproportionately small stall operators, whose costs, satisfaction and needs differ from those of larger e-mail-registered tenants. Excluding them creates coverage error — a mismatch between the sampling frame and the population — which biases every estimate toward the larger-tenant experience. The remedy is to repair the frame, typically by adding a second contact mode for the uncovered group. Where that is impossible, the honest alternative is to redefine the population as “tenants with e-mail on file” and to report it that way, rather than to claim a result about all tenants.

18
A consultant defends a study built on a mall-intercept convenience sample: “We collected 50,000 responses. At that size the Central Limit Theorem guarantees our estimate is accurate.”What is wrong with the appeal to the CLT?
AThe CLT applies only to normally distributed populations, and spending is skewed.
BThe CLT is valid here, but the consultant should also report the standard deviation.
CThe CLT describes the behaviour of x̄ around the mean of the population actually being sampled, under a probability design. It addresses sampling error only; a convenience sample's bias shifts the centre of the sampling distribution, and no sample size corrects that.
DThe CLT requires n below 5% of the population, and 50,000 will exceed that threshold.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. The CLT describes the behaviour of x̄ around the mean of the population actually being sampled, under a probability design. It addresses sampling error only; a convenience sample's bias shifts the centre of the sampling distribution, and no sample size corrects that.

The CLT is a theorem about sampling variability. Its conclusion — that x̄ is approximately normal with mean μ and spread σ/√n — presupposes that the observations are a probability sample from the population of interest. A mall intercept over-represents people who visit malls, visit at that hour, and agree to stop. The sampling distribution of x̄ is then centred not on the population mean but on the mean of that self-selecting group. At n = 50,000 the standard error is minuscule, so the estimate is precise and wrong simultaneously — the most dangerous combination in applied statistics, because narrow intervals read as authority.

19
A researcher divides 60 branches into 6 geographic regions, randomly selects 2 whole regions, and surveys every branch in them. She describes the design as “stratified sampling with 2 strata selected.”What is the correct classification and its consequence?
AIt is stratified sampling, and the estimate will be more precise than an SRS of the same size.
BIt is cluster sampling. Stratified sampling draws from every stratum; cluster sampling selects whole groups and surveys within them. The consequence is that branches within a region resemble one another, so the effective information is less than the raw n suggests and the true standard error is larger than the SRS formula gives.
CIt is systematic sampling with k = 3, and the estimate is unbiased.
DIt is multistage sampling, and no standard error can be computed for it.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. It is cluster sampling. Stratified sampling draws from every stratum; cluster sampling selects whole groups and surveys within them. The consequence is that branches within a region resemble one another, so the effective information is less than the raw n suggests and the true standard error is larger than the SRS formula gives.

The distinguishing question is simple: do you sample from every group, or do you sample the groups themselves? Stratified sampling draws units from all strata, deliberately making the sample look like the population on the stratifying variable — which reduces the standard error. Cluster sampling picks whole groups and accepts whatever is inside them. Here four of six regions are entirely absent, so this is cluster sampling. Because branches in one region share local economic conditions, the responses are correlated, and treating the 20 branches as 20 independent observations understates the true standard error. Cluster designs remain unbiased and are often the only affordable option, but their precision per respondent is lower, and the analysis must account for it.

20
A quality team currently samples 100 units per batch and reports a standard error of 0.80 g on mean fill weight. The plant manager asks what happens if the sample rises to 400 units.What is the new standard error, and what does the answer illustrate?
A0.80 g — the standard error does not change, since σ is a property of the population.
B0.20 g — the standard error is inversely proportional to n.
C0.64 g — the new standard error is the square of the old one.
D0.40 g — quadrupling n halves the SE, because the SE falls with √n.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. 0.40 g — quadrupling n halves the SE, because the SE falls with √n.

SE = σ/√n, so multiplying n by 4 divides the SE by √4 = 2: 0.80/2 = 0.40 g. Equivalently, σ = 0.80 × √100 = 8 g, so at n = 400 the SE is 8/√400 = 8/20 = 0.40 g. The lesson is the shape of the curve: the first hundred units bought a large reduction in uncertainty, the next three hundred buy the same reduction again, and the following twelve hundred would be needed to buy it a third time. Precision has sharply diminishing returns, and there is always a point where tightening the estimate costs more than the decision it informs is worth.

🏬 Case: Habitat Malls Group — the expansion research report

Background. Habitat Malls Group operates 14 shopping centres across the Philippines, recording roughly 2.4 million customer visits per year and leasing to 1,320 tenant businesses, of which 1,240 are retail and food-and-beverage operators — the tenants for whom mall foot traffic is the relevant commercial driver. The board is deciding whether to commit ₱1.8 billion to a fifteenth mall. A newly formed research unit has produced the report below. All the raw figures are accurate as collected; what you are auditing is how they were gathered and how they were used.

#StudyHow the data were collectedWhat was found
AShopper spend surveyInterviewers stationed at the main atrium of the three largest NCR malls, Saturdays 1–5 p.m., approaching shoppers who appeared willing. 480 completed.x̄ = ₱3,850 per visit, s = ₱2,900
BTenant satisfaction surveySimple random sample of 400 drawn from the complete list of the 1,240 retail and F&B tenants; 400 responded.p̂ = 0.68 satisfied with foot traffic
CTenant renewal intentionE-mail invitation across two rounds to all 1,320 current tenant businesses. 480 completed questionnaires.71% intend to renew
DLuxury segment sub-analysisThe 12 respondents in study A who self-identified as luxury buyers. Their spending is heavily right-skewed.x̄ = ₱18,400 per visit
EPrecision plan for round twoBoard has asked for a margin of error on mean spend of ±₱100.Unit proposes n = 960

The research unit's report, verbatim:

A. Average spend per visit is ₱3,850. With a standard error of ₱2,900, we are 95% confident the national average lies between −₱1,834 and ₱9,534. Applied to 2.4 million visits, annual shopper spend is therefore ₱9.24 billion.

B. 68% of tenants are satisfied with foot traffic. The standard error is √(0.68 × 0.32 / 400) = 0.02332, so the margin of error is ±4.57 percentage points.

C. 480 tenants responded and 71% intend to renew. This is a large sample, so the finding is reliable. To be even more certain next year we will widen the invitation list to 3,000 by adding every business that has ever enquired about leasing with us.

D. Luxury buyers spend ₱18,400 per visit on average, with a 95% interval of ₱16,759 to ₱20,041. We recommend allocating 30% of the new mall's gross leasable area to luxury tenants.

E. The current margin of error on mean spend is about ±₱259. To reach ±₱100 we will double the sample to 960.

F. Because the atrium locations were chosen without any preference for particular shoppers, and because interviewers approached people at random intervals, study A constitutes a random sample of Habitat shoppers nationally.

Of the three studies that actually collected data from a sample — A, B and C — only one rests on a sound sampling design. And not one of the six numbered conclusions in the memo is correct. Your job is to identify the sound design and to find the fault in each of the six conclusions. Be careful with the sound study: its fault is an omission that makes it look weaker than it is, which is easy to read past.

Essay 1 · 20 points · Analyse & Evaluate
Audit the design of studies A, B and C, and evaluate claim F. For each, name the sampling method actually used, classify it as probability or non-probability, identify the specific error family involved, and state the direction in which the resulting estimate is likely to be wrong. Then explain why increasing the sample size would or would not help in each case.
Show answer key — what a strong response must establish
  • A named design for each study, with the feature of the description that identifies it.
  • The distinction between sampling error (shrinks with n) and non-sampling error (does not) applied study by study.
