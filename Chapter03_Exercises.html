Twenty multiple-choice items. Most turn on a judgement, not a formula. Choose your answer, then use the dropdown to check it and read why.

20 items
1
A retail analyst writes: “Being a first-time buyer and paying by e-wallet are mutually exclusive, so they must be independent.”What is wrong with this reasoning?
ANothing — mutually exclusive events are a special case of independent events.
BMutually exclusive means the two cannot occur together, so knowing one occurred tells you the other definitely did not — which is maximum dependence, the opposite of independence.
CThe events are independent, but only if their probabilities are equal.
DThe reasoning is fine, but it should have said “collectively exhaustive” instead.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. Mutually exclusive means the two cannot occur together, so knowing one occurred tells you the other definitely did not — which is maximum dependence, the opposite of independence.

The two ideas are close to opposites. Mutually exclusive means P(A ∩ B) = 0; independent means P(A ∩ B) = P(A)·P(B). If both events have positive probability, then P(A)·P(B) > 0 ≠ 0, so the two conditions cannot hold together. Intuitively: if learning that A happened tells you with certainty that B did not, that is the strongest possible dependence. Note that the analyst's premise is also questionable — a first-time buyer can obviously pay by e-wallet — so the claim fails twice over.

2
A quality inspector draws a sample of 20 units from a large production run in which 3% of units are defective. She wants the probability that at least one unit in her sample is defective.Which computation is correct?
A20 × 0.03 × 0.97 = 0.582
B20 × 0.03 = 0.600
C1 − (0.97)²⁰ = 1 − 0.5438 = 0.4562
D(0.03)²⁰, which is essentially zero
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. 1 − (0.97)²⁰ = 1 − 0.5438 = 0.4562

“At least one” is the complement of “none”. P(no defectives) = (0.97)²⁰ = 0.5438, so P(at least one) = 1 − 0.5438 = 0.4562. One line instead of twenty. This is the complement rule doing exactly the job it exists for — and note the result is a genuinely useful number: even at a 3% defect rate, a sample of 20 catches at least one defective less than half the time, which is why acceptance sampling plans need larger samples than managers expect.

3
A batch contains 20 units, 4 of which are defective. Two units are drawn without replacement for inspection.What is the probability that both are defective, and why does it differ from the with-replacement answer?
A0.2000, since the defect rate is 4/20 and it applies to both draws.
B0.0400, because with a batch this large the finite-population effect is negligible.
C0.0400, and it does not differ — drawing two units is the same either way.
D0.0316, which is about 21% lower than the with-replacement value of 0.0400, because removing the first defective reduces both the count and the pool.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. 0.0316, which is about 21% lower than the with-replacement value of 0.0400, because removing the first defective reduces both the count and the pool.

Without replacement the second probability is conditional on the first: P(both) = (4/20)(3/19) = 0.2 × 0.15789 = 0.03158. With replacement it would be (4/20)² = 0.0400. The without-replacement figure is lower because removing a defective leaves only 3 defectives in a pool of 19. The general lesson: sampling without replacement destroys independence, and the effect matters whenever the sample is a meaningful fraction of the population — here 2 of 20, or 10%.

4
A bank's marketing head reports: “Eighty per cent of customers who took our new loan product had visited the branch in the previous month. Branch visits clearly drive loan uptake, so we should increase foot traffic.”What is the flaw?
AThe 80% is P(visited | took loan), but the decision requires P(took loan | visited) — a different quantity that could be very small if most visitors take no loan.
BThe sample size is not reported, so no conclusion is possible.
CThe claim confuses mutually exclusive events with independent ones.
DThere is no flaw; 80% is a strong enough association to justify the action.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. The 80% is P(visited | took loan), but the decision requires P(took loan | visited) — a different quantity that could be very small if most visitors take no loan.

The reported figure conditions on taking the loan and looks backwards at behaviour. The decision needs the forward conditional: of the customers who visit, what share take a loan? If 50,000 customers visit monthly and 400 take the loan, then P(loan | visit) is under 1% even though 80% of borrowers visited. Driving foot traffic on the strength of the reversed conditional could be one of the least efficient interventions available. This is the same P(A|B) versus P(B|A) error as the fraud filter, in a friendlier disguise.

5
A logistics firm receives an average of 3 damage claims per day. Management asks for the probability of a two-day weekend with no claims at all. Claims arise independently.Which computation is correct?
Ae⁻³ = 0.0498, since the rate is 3 per day
Be⁻⁶ = 0.0025, because λ must be rescaled to 6 for a two-day interval
C(e⁻³)² × 2 = 0.0050
D2 × e⁻³ = 0.0996
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. e⁻⁶ = 0.0025, because λ must be rescaled to 6 for a two-day interval

λ is tied to an interval. Over two days the expected number of claims is 6, so λ = 6 and P(X = 0) = e⁻⁶ = 0.00248. Using the daily λ gives 0.0498 — twenty times too large, and the single most common Poisson error. Note that squaring the one-day probability, (e⁻³)² = e⁻⁶, happens to give the same answer here because the days are independent; that is a legitimate alternative route, but only if you square rather than double.

6
A operations analyst models daily IT helpdesk tickets as Poisson. Over 90 days the observed mean is 8.1 tickets and the observed variance is 34.6.What should she conclude?
AThe model fits, but λ should be set to the average of 8.1 and 34.6.
BThe Poisson model fits well, since the mean is close to a whole number.
CThe data is overdispersed — variance far exceeds the mean — so the independence assumption has failed and Poisson is the wrong model.
DThe variance should be ignored, because the Poisson has only one parameter.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. The data is overdispersed — variance far exceeds the mean — so the independence assumption has failed and Poisson is the wrong model.

A Poisson distribution has mean equal to variance. Here the variance is more than four times the mean, which is decisive evidence against it. The usual business cause is clustering: a single system outage generates dozens of tickets at once, so arrivals are not independent — condition 3 fails. Recognising this is valuable, because it means the model would badly understate the probability of a very heavy day, which is exactly the day staffing needs to cover.

7
A recruiter states: “We interview 15 candidates and each has a 20% chance of being hired, so the number hired is binomial with n = 15 and p = 0.20.” In fact the firm has exactly 3 vacancies and stops interviewing once they are filled.Which binomial condition fails?
ATwo outcomes per trial
BNone — the model is appropriate
CConstant probability of success on each trial
DA fixed number of trials decided in advance
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. A fixed number of trials decided in advance

Because interviewing stops once three vacancies are filled, the number of trials is not fixed — it is itself random, determined by how quickly successes accumulate. Condition 1 has failed. When the number of successes is fixed and the number of trials is random, the situation is the mirror image of the binomial (a negative binomial), and applying the binomial formula would misstate the probabilities. Note also a second, subtler failure: with only 3 vacancies the later candidates' hire probabilities depend on earlier outcomes, so independence is compromised too.

8
An analyst must model the number of customers arriving at a branch between 10am and 11am. She has historical data showing an average of 47 arrivals in that hour.Which distribution is appropriate, and what is the decisive reason?
APoisson, because there is an interval and a rate but no countable set of non-arrivals.
BNormal, because 47 is a large number and large counts are normally distributed.
CNone of the three — arrival counts require a distribution not covered in this chapter.
DBinomial, because each minute of the hour either has an arrival or does not.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. Poisson, because there is an interval and a rate but no countable set of non-arrivals.

Apply the decisive test: can you count the non-events? You cannot say how many customers failed to arrive between 10am and 11am, because there was never a defined set of arrival opportunities. There is an interval (one hour) and a rate (λ = 47), which is the Poisson signature. Binomial requires a fixed n with countable failures; that structure simply does not exist here.

9
A coffee chain's delivery times are approximately normal with μ = 42 minutes and σ = 6 minutes.What proportion of deliveries take between 48 and 54 minutes?
A0.8185
B0.1359
C0.1587
D0.0228
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. 0.1359

z₁ = (48 − 42)/6 = +1.00 and z₂ = (54 − 42)/6 = +2.00. Then P(1 < z < 2) = Φ(2.00) − Φ(1.00) = 0.9772 − 0.8413 = 0.1359. Both boundaries are above the mean, so both z-values are positive and you subtract the smaller cumulative area from the larger.

10
A filling machine dispenses coffee beans with mean 250 g and standard deviation 8 g, normally distributed. Regulations require that no more than 2.5% of bags exceed the stated upper limit.What upper limit should be printed on the bag?
A258.00 g
B263.16 g
C265.68 g
D270.64 g
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. 265.68 g

This is a backward problem. If only 2.5% may exceed the limit, then 97.5% must fall below it, so we need z with Φ(z) = 0.9750, which is z = 1.96. Then x = μ + zσ = 250 + 1.96(8) = 250 + 15.68 = 265.68 g. Note the direction of reasoning: the tail probability given is the upper tail, so convert it to a cumulative area before looking up z.

11
A binomial variable has n = 200 and p = 0.15. An analyst wants P(X ≥ 40) using the normal approximation.Which value should be standardised?
A40.5, because the continuity correction always adds 0.5
B39, because the correction moves to the previous whole number
C40, because that is the value in the question
D39.5, because the value 40 is included and the boundary must move outward by half a unit
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. 39.5, because the value 40 is included and the boundary must move outward by half a unit

The bar representing X = 40 spans 39.5 to 40.5 under the continuous curve. Since “at least 40” includes 40, the whole bar belongs in the region, so the boundary moves outward to 39.5. Then z = (39.5 − 30)/5.0498 = 1.88 and the answer is 1 − 0.9699 = 0.0301, against an exact binomial value of 0.0335. Standardising 40 instead gives 0.0238 — an error about 2.8 times larger.

12
An analyst wants P(X ≥ 5) for a binomial with n = 500 and p = 0.006. She proposes the normal approximation with μ = 3 and σ = √(500 × 0.006 × 0.994) = 1.73.Evaluate her approach.
AUnsound — np = 3, which fails the np ≥ 5 condition, so the binomial is still strongly right-skewed. The Poisson with λ = np = 3 is the appropriate approximation here.
BUnsound, because the normal approximation may never be used when p is below 0.5.
CSound, provided she also applies the continuity correction.
DSound — n = 500 is large, which is what the approximation requires.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. Unsound — np = 3, which fails the np ≥ 5 condition, so the binomial is still strongly right-skewed. The Poisson with λ = np = 3 is the appropriate approximation here.

Both conditions must hold, and np = 500(0.006) = 3, which is below 5. Large n is not sufficient — with p this small the distribution remains crushed against zero and a symmetric curve cannot fit it. The right tool is the Poisson with λ = np = 3, which is designed for large n with small p. In this instance Poisson gives P(X ≥ 5) = 0.1847 against an exact binomial value of 0.1842 — accurate to three decimal places, where the normal approximation would perform poorly.

13
Two mutually exclusive investments are compared.
Project A — E(X) = ₱2.0M, σ = ₱0.4M
Project B — E(X) = ₱2.2M, σ = ₱11.4M, with a 20% chance of losing ₱20M. The firm's total equity is ₱15M.Which recommendation is most defensible?
AProject B, because it has the higher expected monetary value.
BProject A, because although its EMV is ₱0.2M lower, Project B carries a 20% chance of a loss that exceeds the firm's entire equity.
CProject B, because a higher standard deviation means greater upside potential.
DNeither can be compared, because expected values are only valid for repeated decisions.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. Project A, because although its EMV is ₱0.2M lower, Project B carries a 20% chance of a loss that exceeds the firm's entire equity.

EMV is the right starting point and the wrong stopping point. Project B's advantage is ₱0.2M — about 9% — while its downside branch would wipe out the firm: a ₱20M loss against ₱15M of equity is insolvency, not a bad quarter. Expected value implicitly assumes you can absorb the worst outcome and repeat the bet enough times for the average to materialise. A firm that cannot survive one draw from the distribution cannot rely on its long-run mean. This is why EMV must always be reported with σ and with an explicit check on the worst branch.

14
A pre-employment integrity test flags dishonest applicants with 90% sensitivity and wrongly flags 5% of honest applicants. In the applicant pool, 4% are genuinely dishonest.Approximately what proportion of flagged applicants are genuinely dishonest?
A5%
B90%
C43%
D4%
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. 43%

P(Flag) = 0.90(0.04) + 0.05(0.96) = 0.036 + 0.048 = 0.084. Then P(Dishonest | Flag) = 0.036/0.084 = 0.4286, about 43%. So the majority of flagged applicants are honest — even at a 4% base rate, which is twenty times higher than the fraud example's 0.2%. The comparison is instructive: raising the base rate from 0.2% to 4% lifted the reversed conditional from 8.7% to 43%, which is why screening tools should be pointed at populations where the base rate is already elevated.

15
A firm must fill three distinct roles — Chief Analyst, Deputy Analyst and Data Steward — from a shortlist of seven candidates. Separately, it must choose three members for an unranked advisory panel from the same seven.How many ways are there in each case?
A35 for the roles and 210 for the panel
B343 for the roles and 21 for the panel
C210 for the roles and 210 for the panel
D210 for the roles and 35 for the panel
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. 210 for the roles and 35 for the panel

The three roles are distinct, so order matters: 7P3 = 7 × 6 × 5 = 210. The panel is unranked, so only membership matters: 7C3 = 210/3! = 35. The ratio is exactly 3! = 6, the number of ways to rearrange any chosen trio among the three roles. The test to apply: if I swap two selected people, is the result different? For roles yes; for a panel no.

16
An analyst reports: “Delivery time is normal with μ = 42 and σ = 6, so the probability a delivery takes exactly 45 minutes is about 0.06.”What is wrong?
AFor a continuous distribution the probability of any single exact value is zero, because a single point encloses no area; the analyst should report an interval such as P(44.5 < X < 45.5).
BNothing is wrong — 0.06 is the correct height of the density curve at 45.
CThe probability should be computed as 1/6, since σ = 6.
DThe arithmetic is wrong; the correct value is about 0.04.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. For a continuous distribution the probability of any single exact value is zero, because a single point encloses no area; the analyst should report an interval such as P(44.5 < X < 45.5).

Probability for a continuous variable is area under the density curve, and a single point has no width and therefore no area. So P(X = 45) = 0 exactly. What the analyst has probably computed is the density at 45, which is a height, not a probability — densities can even exceed 1 for tightly concentrated variables. The professional statement is an interval. Note the useful corollary: because single points carry zero probability, P(X ≤ 45) = P(X < 45), which is why continuous problems need no endpoint fussing — unlike the discrete case in §3.10.

17
A supplier's on-time delivery rate is 92%. Over the next 30 shipments, an analyst wants the probability that at least 25 arrive on time, and computes it as a binomial. A colleague objects that “30 shipments is too small for probability to work.”Evaluate the objection.
AThe colleague is right — probability calculations need at least 30 observations.
BThe colleague is wrong. The binomial is exact for any n and needs no minimum; a minimum applies only to the normal approximation, and here nq = 2.4 means that approximation should be avoided.
CThe colleague is right, but only because p is above 0.9.
DThe colleague is wrong, and the normal approximation may be used freely since n = 30.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. The colleague is wrong. The binomial is exact for any n and needs no minimum; a minimum applies only to the normal approximation, and here nq = 2.4 means that approximation should be avoided.

Two distinct ideas are being conflated. The binomial formula is exact — it gives the correct probability for n = 3 or n = 3,000, with no sample-size requirement. Minimum-size conditions attach to approximations. And here the approximation would be a poor choice for a reason worth noticing: np = 27.6 comfortably clears 5, but nq = 30(0.08) = 2.4 does not, so the distribution is strongly left-skewed. Compute the binomial directly.

18
An analyst computes the probability that a customer both subscribes to the newsletter and makes a purchase as P(N) × P(B) = 0.30 × 0.20 = 0.06. Company records show that 12% of customers do both.What does the discrepancy tell you?
ANothing — a difference of 6 percentage points is within normal sampling variation.
BOne of the two individual probabilities must be wrong.
CThe events are not independent — the actual joint probability is double the independence prediction, so P(B | N) = 0.12/0.30 = 0.40 rather than 0.20.
DThe records must be wrong, since 0.06 follows from the multiplication rule.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. The events are not independent — the actual joint probability is double the independence prediction, so P(B | N) = 0.12/0.30 = 0.40 rather than 0.20.

The multiplication shortcut P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B) is valid only for independent events. Observing 0.12 where independence predicts 0.06 is direct evidence that the events are dependent, and it lets you recover the conditional: P(B | N) = P(N ∩ B)/P(N) = 0.12/0.30 = 0.40. Newsletter subscribers buy at twice the base rate. That is a finding, not an error — and it would have been erased by assuming independence.

19
A student writes: “Since the empirical rule says 95% of values lie within two standard deviations, and our claim amounts have mean ₱4,200 and standard deviation ₱3,800, about 95% of claims fall between −₱3,400 and ₱11,800.” Claim amounts are strongly right-skewed.Which criticism is most complete?
AThe arithmetic is wrong — two standard deviations either side of ₱4,200 gives ₱400 to ₱8,000.
BThe rule applies, but only after the claim amounts have been converted to z-scores.
CThe interval should have been computed with three standard deviations rather than two.
DThe empirical rule is a property of the normal curve only; for skewed data it can be badly wrong, and the negative lower bound is itself the tell that a symmetric model has been forced onto an asymmetric variable. Chebyshev's theorem would give a valid but looser guarantee of at least 75%.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. The empirical rule is a property of the normal curve only; for skewed data it can be badly wrong, and the negative lower bound is itself the tell that a symmetric model has been forced onto an asymmetric variable. Chebyshev's theorem would give a valid but looser guarantee of at least 75%.

The 68–95–99.7 figures are the areas Φ(1) − Φ(−1), Φ(2) − Φ(−2) and Φ(3) − Φ(−3) of the normal curve, as Figure 3.11 shows. They carry no general validity. The negative lower bound is diagnostic: claim amounts cannot be below zero, so a symmetric interval has produced an impossible region — which by itself should stop the analysis. Chebyshev's theorem assumes nothing about shape and guarantees at least 1 − 1/2² = 75% within two standard deviations.

20
An e-commerce firm wants to deploy an automated model that blocks suspected fraudulent orders. The model has 93% sensitivity and a 3% false-positive rate. Company-wide, 0.4% of orders are fraudulent, but among orders that are shipping to a newly-added address and paid with a card issued abroad, the fraud rate is 11%.Which deployment design makes the best use of the model?
AApply it only to the elevated-risk segment and route flags to human review rather than automatic blocking, because the reversed conditional is far more favourable where the base rate is higher.
BApply it to all orders but lower the sensitivity, which will reduce the number of false positives.
CDo not deploy it — a 3% false-positive rate is too high for any use.
DApply it to all orders and block every flagged order automatically, since 93% sensitivity is high.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. Apply it only to the elevated-risk segment and route flags to human review rather than automatic blocking, because the reversed conditional is far more favourable where the base rate is higher.

Run both numbers. Company-wide: P(Flag) = 0.93(0.004) + 0.03(0.996) = 0.00372 + 0.02988 = 0.0336, so P(Fraud | Flag) = 0.00372/0.0336 = 11% — nine of ten blocked orders would be legitimate. In the elevated segment: P(Flag) = 0.93(0.11) + 0.03(0.89) = 0.1023 + 0.0267 = 0.129, so P(Fraud | Flag) = 0.1023/0.129 = 79%. Same model, seven-fold improvement in precision, purely from where it is pointed. Human review on top is what keeps the residual 21% of false positives from becoming lost customers.

🛵 Case: Padala Express — the quarterly operations memo

Background. Padala Express runs last-mile parcel delivery across Metro Manila using a pool of contracted motorcycle riders. A newly hired operations analyst has prepared a memo covering four operational questions and one strategic one. The underlying figures below are all correct; the analyst's use of them is what you are auditing.

#QuantityWhat the historical data saysQuestion management asked
ARider no-shows40 riders are scheduled per shift; 8% independently fail to log inHow likely is a shift with 8 or more no-shows? (8 is the point at which service degrades.)
BParcel damage claimsAn average of 2.5 claims per day, arising from independent causesWhat is the probability of a completely claim-free week?
CDelivery timeApproximately normal, μ = 38 min, σ = 7 minWhat delivery window can we advertise and meet 90% of the time?
DRisky-rider screening modelFlags 88% of riders who will cause a serious incident within six months; wrongly flags 6% of those who will not. Historically 3% of riders cause such an incident.If the model flags a rider, how confident can we be that they are genuinely high-risk?
ECebu expansionStrong demand p = 0.30 → +₱15M · Moderate p = 0.45 → +₱4M · Weak p = 0.25 → −₱20M. Company equity is ₱16M.Should we proceed?

The analyst's memo, verbatim:

A. With 40 riders and an 8% no-show rate, no-shows are normally distributed with μ = 3.2 and σ = 1.72. So P(X ≥ 8) = P(z > 2.80) = 0.0026. A degraded shift is a once-in-400-shifts event and needs no contingency planning.

B. Claims follow a Poisson distribution with λ = 2.5. The probability of a claim-free week is therefore e−2.5 = 0.082, or about one week in twelve.

C. Using z = 1.28 for 90%, the advertised window should be 38 + 1.28(7) = 46.96, so 47 minutes.

D. The screening model is 88% accurate, so when it flags a rider we can be 88% confident the rider is genuinely high-risk. I recommend automatically deactivating every flagged rider.

E. The Cebu expansion has an expected monetary value of +₱1.3M. Since the EMV is positive, we should proceed.

F. Separately, note that rider no-shows and heavy rainfall are independent events, so the probability of both occurring on a given day is simply P(no-show) × P(rain).

Exactly one of the five substantive answers is both correctly computed and correctly concluded. Your job is to find out which. Be careful: one other item is arithmetically right but reaches an indefensible conclusion, and saying so is part of a complete audit.

  • A practical consequence for each error, not just a corrected number.
Essay 2 · 20 points · Apply & Create

Explain in language a non-statistician operations director would accept why the answer is so far from 88%. Then design a defensible deployment of the model, and state what would have to change for automatic deactivation to become justifiable.
Show answer key — what a strong response must establish
  • The full Bayes computation, and identification of what "88%" actually is.
  • A whole-number confusion matrix — this is what convinces a non-statistician.
  • The mechanism: a small false-positive rate applied to a large clean population.
  • Use of the other conditional, P(risky | not flagged), which is where the model's real value lies.
  • A deployment design, plus the specific conditions under which the analyst's recommendation would become sound.

Show answer key — what a strong response must establish
  • ΣP(x) = 1 checked before anything else.
