20 items
1
A report states: “Mean handle time is 312 seconds, 95% CI [303.18, 320.82]. There is therefore a 95% probability that the true mean lies between 303.18 and 320.82 seconds.”What is wrong with the second sentence?
Aμ is a fixed number and both endpoints are now fixed numbers, so the statement is either true or false — there is no probability left in it. The 95% describes how often the method captures μ across repeated samples.
BThe probability should be stated as 97.5%, because the interval is two-sided.
CNothing — that is the standard definition of a confidence interval.
DIt should say 95% of calls last between those two values.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. μ is a fixed number and both endpoints are now fixed numbers, so the statement is either true or false — there is no probability left in it. The 95% describes how often the method captures μ across repeated samples.

The population mean is an unknown constant, not a random variable, and once you have computed [303.18, 320.82] the endpoints are constants too. Either μ is between them or it is not. What varies from sample to sample is the interval: 95% of the intervals a method like this produces would contain μ. The correct phrasing is “we are 95% confident”, where confidence is a technical claim about the procedure. Note that a Bayesian credible interval does legitimately support a probability statement — but it requires a prior distribution and is a different calculation.

2
A random sample of 25 invoices has a mean processing time of 48 minutes with a sample standard deviation of 6 minutes. The population standard deviation is unknown.What is the 95% confidence interval for the mean processing time?
A[46.80, 49.20]
B[45.52, 50.48]
C[45.65, 50.35]
D[36.24, 59.76]
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. [45.52, 50.48]

σ is unknown, so this is a t-interval with df = 25 − 1 = 24. SE = 6/√25 = 6/5 = 1.20. The two-sided 95% value at df = 24 is t = 2.064, so E = 2.064 × 1.20 = 2.477 and the interval is 48 ± 2.48 = [45.52, 50.48]. Sanity check: the t multiplier must exceed 1.96, and 2.064 does.

3
An analyst has 12 observations, computes s from the data, and builds a 95% interval using the multiplier 1.96.What is the consequence, and why?
AThe interval is too wide, because t would have been smaller than z at df = 11.
BThe interval is invalid because 12 observations can never support an interval.
CThe interval is too narrow. With σ unknown the multiplier should be t at df = 11, which is 2.201 — about 12% larger than 1.96 — so the reported precision is overstated.
DNo consequence — 1.96 is correct for any 95% interval.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. The interval is too narrow. With σ unknown the multiplier should be t at df = 11, which is 2.201 — about 12% larger than 1.96 — so the reported precision is overstated.

Replacing σ with s introduces a second source of randomness, and the t distribution compensates with heavier tails and a larger multiplier. At df = 11 the two-sided 95% value is 2.201, so using 1.96 shrinks the margin by roughly 11% and the interval is correspondingly too narrow. The direction matters: the analyst is claiming more precision than the data supports, which is the error that leads to overconfident decisions. At n = 12 the shortfall is material; at n = 100 it would be under 1.3%, which is why the shortcut is harmless at large n and still not worth taking.

4
From n = 480 shoppers, with the population standard deviation known from a prior transaction census to be σ = ₱2,900, a 95% confidence interval for mean monthly spend is [₱3,591, ₱4,109]. A manager writes: “So 95% of our customers spend between ₱3,591 and ₱4,109 a month.”How should this be assessed?
AIt is wrong because confidence intervals never describe customers, only samples.
BIt is a reasonable paraphrase for a non-technical audience.
CIt is correct only if spending is normally distributed.
DIt is wrong. The interval estimates the mean, whose standard error is σ/√n; the spread of individual customers is σ, which is √n times larger. At n = 480 and σ = ₱2,900, individual spending spans thousands of pesos, not hundreds.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. It is wrong. The interval estimates the mean, whose standard error is σ/√n; the spread of individual customers is σ, which is √n times larger. At n = 480 and σ = ₱2,900, individual spending spans thousands of pesos, not hundreds.

This is the second of the three classic misreadings, and the width itself gives it away: an interval only ₱518 wide cannot be describing individual customers whose standard deviation is ₱2,900. The interval concerns μ, and its half-width is 1.96 × 2900/√480 = ₱259. A range covering roughly 95% of individuals would be about 3,850 ± 1.96(2,900) — several thousand pesos wide, and mostly meaningless here because spending is skewed. Whenever an interval feels suspiciously narrow for a statement about people, you are probably reading a confidence interval as if it were a data range.

5
A research unit must estimate the proportion of customers who will switch to a new plan, with a 95% margin of error of ±4 percentage points. No prior estimate of the proportion exists.What sample size is required?
A25
B601
C385
D1,068
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. 601

With no prior information, use p = 0.5, which maximises p(1−p) and therefore guarantees the sample is large enough whatever the truth turns out to be. n = 0.25 × (1.96/0.04)² = 0.25 × (49)² = 0.25 × 2,401 = 600.25 → round up to 601. Verify: 1.96√(0.25/601) = 0.0400 ✓. Rounding down to 600 would leave the margin a hair above the promised ±4 points, which is the one direction the calculation exists to prevent.

6
For n = 20 and s = 4.2, an analyst computes the 95% interval for σ² as (19 × 17.64)/8.907 for the lower bound and (19 × 17.64)/32.852 for the upper bound, obtaining “[37.63, 10.20]”.What has gone wrong?
ANothing is wrong; chi-square intervals are conventionally written largest-first.
BThe degrees of freedom should be 20, not 19.
CThe two chi-square cut-offs are the wrong way round. Dividing by the larger value (32.852) produces the lower bound and dividing by the smaller (8.907) produces the upper, so the correct interval is [10.20, 37.63].
DThe numerator should be n·s² = 20 × 17.64, not (n − 1)s².
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. The two chi-square cut-offs are the wrong way round. Dividing by the larger value (32.852) produces the lower bound and dividing by the smaller (8.907) produces the upper, so the correct interval is [10.20, 37.63].

The formula is (n−1)s²/χ²right < σ² < (n−1)s²/χ²left, and the crossover is arithmetic rather than convention: dividing a fixed numerator by a bigger denominator gives a smaller result. So 335.16/32.852 = 10.202 is the lower bound and 335.16/8.907 = 37.629 is the upper. The self-check is built in — if your “lower” bound comes out above your “upper”, you have swapped them. Taking square roots gives the interval for σ: [3.194, 6.134].

7
A director reviews two reports on the same data. Report 1 gives a 95% interval of [60.2%, 67.8%]. Report 2 gives a 99% interval of [59.0%, 69.0%]. The director concludes that Report 2 is “more accurate” and should be used.How should this reasoning be assessed?
ACorrect — a higher confidence level means a more reliable estimate.
BCorrect, because the wider interval is more likely to contain the true value.
CIncorrect, because the 99% interval should have been narrower than the 95% one.
DIncorrect. The 99% interval is wider, therefore less informative. It is right more often but says less; taken to the limit, a 100% interval spans every possible value and is worthless.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. Incorrect. The 99% interval is wider, therefore less informative. It is right more often but says less; taken to the limit, a 100% interval spans every possible value and is worthless.

Confidence and precision are traded against each other, not accumulated. Raising the level from 95% to 99% changes the multiplier from 1.96 to 2.576 and widens the interval by about 31% on identical data. The wider interval does contain μ more often — that much is true — but each extra percentage point of confidence is paid for in width, and width is exactly the information the reader wanted. Neither report is more accurate; they are the same estimate stated with different tolerances for being wrong. Raise the level when the cost of an error is severe and asymmetric, such as a safety limit or a regulatory threshold; otherwise 95% is the professional default.

8
In a random sample of 400 subscribers, 140 said they would recommend the service.What is the 95% confidence interval for the population proportion?
A[0.3033, 0.3967]
B[0.3268, 0.3732]
C[0.2565, 0.4435]
D[0.3396, 0.3604]
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. [0.3033, 0.3967]

p̂ = 140/400 = 0.35. Check the condition: np̂ = 140 and n(1−p̂) = 260, both far above 5 ✓. SE = √[0.35 × 0.65/400] = √0.00056875 = 0.023848. E = 1.96 × 0.023848 = 0.046743, so the interval is 0.35 ± 0.0467 = [0.3033, 0.3967], or 30.3% to 39.7% — a margin of ±4.7 percentage points.

9
A board will approve a product launch if adoption exceeds 55%. A pilot suggests adoption is near 64%. The research manager proposes a survey sized for a 95% margin of error of ±1 percentage point, requiring n ≈ 8,900.What is the strongest professional response?
AApprove the plan; the tighter the margin, the better the evidence.
BHalve the budget by lowering the confidence level to 90% and keeping n at 8,900.
CAsk what precision the decision requires. The threshold is 9 points from the estimate, so a ±4-point margin (n ≈ 554 using p̂ = 0.64) would place the entire interval above 55% and answer the question at a fraction of the cost.
DReject any survey and rely on the pilot, since 64% already exceeds 55%.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. Ask what precision the decision requires. The threshold is 9 points from the estimate, so a ±4-point margin (n ≈ 554 using p̂ = 0.64) would place the entire interval above 55% and answer the question at a fraction of the cost.

Sample size should be derived backwards from the decision. The board's threshold is 55% and the estimate is 64%, so the question is whether the interval can be kept clear of 55%. With p̂ = 0.64 and E = 0.04, n = 0.64 × 0.36 × (1.96/0.04)² = 0.2304 × 2,401 = 553.2 → n = 554, and the resulting interval of roughly [60%, 68%] sits entirely above the threshold. Going to ±1 point costs sixteen times the sample to answer the same question with the same conclusion. Precision beyond what the decision needs is waste, and identifying that is the analyst's job rather than the board's.

10
A franchisor's contractual food-cost target is 33.0%. An audit of 15 branches gives a 95% interval of [31.61%, 33.39%]. The operations head writes: “We are running 0.5 points under target, so we can relax controls.”What is the flaw in that conclusion?
AThe conclusion is sound but should be stated as a 0.5-point saving with a margin of error.
BThe interval should have been computed at 99% before drawing any conclusion.
CThe sample of 15 is too small for any conclusion to be drawn.
D33.0% lies inside the interval, so the data cannot distinguish the true food cost from the target. The point estimate of 32.5% is consistent with a true value of 33.2% just as much as with 32.0%, and no difference has been demonstrated.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. 33.0% lies inside the interval, so the data cannot distinguish the true food cost from the target. The point estimate of 32.5% is consistent with a true value of 33.2% just as much as with 32.0%, and no difference has been demonstrated.

The whole purpose of the interval is to say which conclusions the data can and cannot support. Because 33.0 falls inside [31.61, 33.39], a true food cost of exactly 33.0% is entirely consistent with this sample — as is 32.0% and 33.3%. Reporting a 0.5-point gap treats a difference the study cannot detect as an established fact, and relaxing controls on that basis risks acting on noise. The defensible statement is that the chain appears to be operating near target, with no evidence of a departure in either direction. Note that this comparison — does the interval contain the claimed value? — is hypothesis testing arrived at from the other direction, which is where Chapter 6 begins.

11
A study with n = 100 produces a 95% confidence interval of width 8 units. The team can afford to quadruple the sample to n = 400. Assume s is unchanged.What will the new interval width be, approximately?
A4 units
B8 units — width does not depend on n
C2 units
D6 units
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. 4 units

The margin of error carries σ/√n, so quadrupling n divides the width by √4 = 2: 8 → 4 units. This is the single most useful planning fact in the chapter — halving the width costs four times the sample. There is a small additional gain from the t multiplier falling (1.984 at df = 99 versus 1.966 at df = 399), but at these sample sizes it is under one percent and the √n relationship carries the answer.

12
An analyst builds a chi-square confidence interval for σ from a sample of n = 250 claim amounts. The distribution of claim amounts is strongly right-skewed. She argues: “n is large, so the Central Limit Theorem makes this valid.”How should the argument be assessed?
ASound, provided the finite population correction is also applied.
BUnsound. The CLT applies to sample means, not to sample variances. The chi-square interval requires an approximately normal population and is not robust to skew at any sample size.
CUnsound, because the chi-square interval requires n ≤ 30.
DSound — n = 250 far exceeds the n ≥ 30 threshold.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. Unsound. The CLT applies to sample means, not to sample variances. The chi-square interval requires an approximately normal population and is not robust to skew at any sample size.

This is the most consequential assumption in the chapter. The CLT guarantees that the sampling distribution of x̄ approaches normality as n grows, whatever the population's shape — which is why t-intervals for a mean are robust. It says nothing about the sampling distribution of s², whose reference distribution is chi-square only when the underlying population is normal. With strongly skewed data, the chi-square interval for σ can be badly wrong at n = 25 and equally wrong at n = 2,500; more data does not repair it. The standard modern remedy is a bootstrap interval, which makes no normality assumption.

13
A sample of 12 delivery times has a standard deviation of 3.5 minutes. The relevant chi-square cut-offs at df = 11 are 3.816 and 21.920.What is the 95% confidence interval for the population standard deviation σ?
A[2.916, 4.084]
B[2.590, 6.207]
C[6.147, 35.314]
D[2.479, 5.943]
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. [2.479, 5.943]

First the variance interval. (n − 1)s² = 11 × 3.5² = 11 × 12.25 = 134.75. Lower bound: 134.75/21.920 = 6.147. Upper bound: 134.75/3.816 = 35.314. Then take square roots for σ: √6.147 = 2.479 and √35.314 = 5.943, giving [2.479, 5.943] minutes. Notice the asymmetry — s = 3.5 sits 1.02 above the lower bound but 2.44 below the upper, because the chi-square distribution is right-skewed.

14
A quality team inspects 80 units and finds 3 defective. They report p̂ = 0.0375 with a 95% interval of [−0.0044, 0.0794].What does the negative lower bound indicate?
AThe normality condition has failed. np̂ = 3, which is below 5, so the symmetric normal-based formula does not apply here and produces an impossible bound.
BA rounding error in the standard error; the bound should be exactly zero.
CThat the defect rate is genuinely zero and the sample was unrepresentative.
DNothing unusual — proportions intervals routinely extend below zero.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. The normality condition has failed. np̂ = 3, which is below 5, so the symmetric normal-based formula does not apply here and produces an impossible bound.

Check the condition before the arithmetic: np̂ = 80 × 0.0375 = 3, comfortably below the threshold of 5, while n(1 − p̂) = 77 passes. Both must hold. When p̂ is extreme and n is modest, the true sampling distribution of p̂ is skewed and cannot be represented by a symmetric interval — which is exactly what the impossible negative bound is telling you. The remedies are to collect more data (n ≈ 134 would bring np̂ to 5 at this rate) or to use a method built for small counts, such as the Wilson or Clopper–Pearson interval, which are asymmetric by construction and cannot produce a negative bound.

15
A marketing director asks an analyst to narrow a ±4-point margin of error without increasing the budget. The analyst quietly recomputes the interval at 90% confidence instead of 95% and reports the narrower result without comment.What is the most serious problem with this response?
ANothing — 90% is a recognised confidence level.
BThe error rate has doubled from 1-in-20 to 1-in-10, and the report no longer states the level clearly, so the reader will compare it against 95% figures and believe the study is more precise than it is.
C90% confidence is never acceptable in business research.
DThe analyst should have raised the confidence level to 99% instead.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. The error rate has doubled from 1-in-20 to 1-in-10, and the report no longer states the level clearly, so the reader will compare it against 95% figures and believe the study is more precise than it is.

Lowering the confidence level is a legitimate choice, but it is a choice about how often you are willing to be wrong, not a technique for getting more from the same data. Moving from 95% to 90% narrows the interval by about 16% and simultaneously doubles the long-run failure rate. Done openly, with the level stated and the reason given, it can be defensible. Done quietly, it manufactures apparent precision — and because most readers assume 95% by default, the narrower interval will be read as a better study rather than a laxer one. The honest options were to improve measurement or stratify, or to explain that ±4 points is what the budget buys and ask what decision the number feeds.

16
A process has a known standard deviation of 12 grams. Management requires a 95% margin of error of ±2 grams on the mean.What sample size is required?
A12
B71
C139
D554
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. 139

n = (zσ/E)² = (1.96 × 12/2)² = (11.76)² = 138.2976 → round up to 139. Verify: 1.96 × 12/√139 = 1.995 g ✓, just inside the target. Rounding down to 138 would give 2.002 g, marginally over the promised margin — which is why the rule is always to round up.

17
You are drafting the results paragraph of a customer research report for a board that is not statistically trained.Which set of elements makes the paragraph professionally complete?
AThe point estimate; the interval with its confidence level stated; the sample size; the sampling design and response rate; and what the interval implies for the decision at hand.
BThe point estimate, the p-value and the sample size.
CThe point estimate, the confidence interval, and a statement that the results are statistically significant.
DThe confidence interval alone, since it contains the point estimate at its centre.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. The point estimate; the interval with its confidence level stated; the sample size; the sampling design and response rate; and what the interval implies for the decision at hand.

Each element does a distinct job. The point estimate is the number that will be quoted onward regardless of what else you write. The interval with its level is uninterpretable without the level, since 95% and 99% intervals look identical on the page. The sample size lets a reader recompute and signals immediately how serious the study was. The design and response rate cover the non-sampling errors that no formula in this chapter can see — omit them and the reader will assume there were none. The decision implication is what distinguishes an analyst from a calculator. A board paragraph containing all five is both honest and actionable.

18
A survey reports: “Satisfaction is 72%, with a 95% confidence interval of 68% to 76%.” A colleague summarises it as “a margin of error of 8 percentage points”.What is the error in that summary?
AThe margin of error is 8 points only at 99% confidence.
BThe summary is correct; the margin of error is the interval's full width.
CThe margin of error is 4 percentage points — half the width. The interval is estimate ± E, so E is the distance from the centre to either endpoint, not the distance between the endpoints.
DThe margin of error cannot be recovered from a reported interval.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. The margin of error is 4 percentage points — half the width. The interval is estimate ± E, so E is the distance from the centre to either endpoint, not the distance between the endpoints.

The interval is written estimate ± E, so E is the half-width: (76 − 68)/2 = 4 percentage points. Equivalently, 72 − 68 = 4 and 76 − 72 = 4. Doubling the margin is a common slip and it makes a study sound half as precise as it is. The check is quick — the point estimate should sit exactly at the midpoint of the interval, and E is its distance to either end. Working backwards further: E = 1.96 × SE gives SE = 0.0204, and solving 1.96√(0.72 × 0.28/n) = 0.04 gives n ≈ 484.

19
Four intervals are reported for a mean customer spend of ₱3,850 from a sample of n = 480, with the population standard deviation known to be σ = ₱2,900. The reports disagree about which confidence level was used.Which interval is arithmetically impossible for a sample of n = 480, whatever confidence level was intended?
A[₱3,509, ₱4,191]
B[₱3,846, ₱3,854]
C[₱3,559, ₱4,141]
D[₱3,591, ₱4,109]
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. [₱3,846, ₱3,854]

SE = 2,900/√480 = ₱132.37, so a 95% margin is 1.96 × 132.37 = ₱259.44 and the interval must be about ₱519 wide. An interval of [3,846, 3,854] is only ₱8 wide, implying a margin of ₱4 — which would require a standard error of ₱2.04 and therefore a sample of roughly 2 million. It cannot come from 480 observations. The general check is worth internalising: compute s/√n mentally, multiply by about 2, and see whether the reported half-width is in the right neighbourhood.

20
A published report gives a 95% confidence interval for mean weekly sales as [₱182,160, ₱197,840] but omits the point estimate and the sample size. The population standard deviation is known to be ₱40,000.What are the point estimate and the sample size?
Ax̄ = ₱190,000 and n = 30
Bx̄ = ₱197,840 and n = 100
Cx̄ = ₱182,160 and n = 50
Dx̄ = ₱190,000 and n = 100
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. x̄ = ₱190,000 and n = 100

The point estimate is the midpoint: (182,160 + 197,840)/2 = ₱190,000. The margin is the half-width: (197,840 − 182,160)/2 = ₱7,840. Now invert the margin formula, using z because σ is known: n = (zσ/E)² = (1.96 × 40,000/7,840)² = (10)² = 100. This reverse-engineering is a genuinely useful audit skill: any symmetric interval yields its point estimate, its margin of error and — given σ — the sample size behind it, which is how you audit a report that omits them.

🛒 Case: Suki Mart — the private-label launch memo

Background. Suki Mart operates 46 supermarkets across Luzon and the Visayas. The board is deciding whether to commit ₱240 million to launching a private-label grocery line. A research unit has produced the memo below. All the raw data were collected competently from probability samples; what you are auditing is the estimation.

#StudyData collectedWhat the memo concluded
ACategory spendRandom sample of 18 store-months drawn from the 552 store-months of the past trading year; mean monthly spend per shopper on the target category: x̄ = ₱1,485, s = ₱620. σ unknown.95% CI reported as [₱1,198.57, ₱1,771.43]
BPurchase intentionRandom sample of 250 shoppers; 155 said they would buy a Suki Mart private label."62% of our shoppers will buy the private label."
CInterpretationSame data as B."There is a 95% probability that the true rate is inside our interval, and 95% of shoppers fall in that range."
DRound-two planBoard requires a 95% margin of error of ±3 percentage points on purchase intention.Unit proposes n = 400
ESupplier consistencyRandom sample of 25 pilot packs; net-weight standard deviation s = 4.1 g. Weights are approximately normal. Supplier's contract promises σ ≤ 3.0 g."s = 4.1 is close to 3.0, so the supplier is broadly compliant."
FConfidence policy—"To be safe, all figures in the final board pack will be reported at 99% confidence rather than 95%, which will make them more accurate."

The research unit's memo, verbatim:

A. Category spend averages ₱1,485 per shopper per month. Using z = 1.96 and a standard error of ₱146.14, the 95% confidence interval is ₱1,198.57 to ₱1,771.43.

B. 62% of shoppers will buy the private label. On 46 stores serving 1.2 million shoppers a month, that is 744,000 buyers, which supports the ₱240 million investment.

C. There is a 95% probability that the true purchase-intention rate lies inside our confidence interval, and 95% of our shoppers fall within that range.

D. The board wants ±3 percentage points. We propose n = 400 for round two, which roughly doubles our current sample and will therefore halve the margin of error.

E. Net-weight variability came in at s = 4.1 g against the supplier's promised 3.0 g. Since 4.1 is close to 3.0, the supplier is broadly compliant and no contractual action is needed.

F. All board-pack figures will be reported at 99% confidence rather than 95%, which will make our estimates more accurate.

Every one of the six conclusions is wrong, and they fail in six different ways: a wrong multiplier, a point estimate reported as certainty, two misinterpretations of what an interval says, a sample-size relationship inverted, a specification tested against the wrong quantity, and confidence mistaken for accuracy. Your job is to identify each failure, correct it, and say what it would have cost the board.
