20 items
1
A pilot at 16 branches tests H₀: μ = 42 against H₁: μ > 42 with σ = 12. The sample mean is 45, giving z = 1.00 and p = 0.159. The memo concludes: “The new service model produced no improvement.”What is the most serious problem with that conclusion?
AFailing to reject H₀ is not evidence that H₀ is true, and this study had only about 26% power to detect a 3-point improvement — so its null result carries almost no information.
BThe conclusion is sound; p = 0.159 is well above 0.05.
CThe pilot should have used a t-test, since 16 branches is a small sample.
DThe test should have been two-tailed, which would have made the result significant.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. Failing to reject H₀ is not evidence that H₀ is true, and this study had only about 26% power to detect a 3-point improvement — so its null result carries almost no information.

With SE = 12/√16 = 3.00, the critical mean is 42 + 1.645(3.00) = 46.93 — the pilot needed a 4.93-point lift just to reach significance. If the true improvement were exactly the 3 points observed, the chance of detecting it was 0.26; even a 5-point improvement would have been caught only about half the time. A study with 26% power fails to reject almost everything, so its failure to reject tells you essentially nothing about the world. Roughly n = 99 would have been needed for 80% power. The honest sentence is “the pilot did not detect a significant improvement, but it was not large enough to have found one.”

2
A random sample of 64 deliveries has a mean of 98 minutes against a target of 100 minutes. The population standard deviation is known to be 8 minutes.Testing H₀: μ = 100 against H₁: μ < 100, what are the test statistic and the p-value?
Az = −16.00, p ≈ 0
Bz = −2.00, p = 0.0228
Cz = −0.25, p = 0.4013
Dz = −2.00, p = 0.0455
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. z = −2.00, p = 0.0228

SE = σ/√n = 8/√64 = 8/8 = 1.00. Then z = (98 − 100)/1.00 = −2.00. The alternative is one-sided, so the p-value is the single left-tail area: P(Z < −2.00) = 0.0228. Since 0.0228 < 0.05, reject H₀ — there is evidence that mean delivery time is below the 100-minute target.

3
A marketing team plans a two-tailed test of H₀: p = 0.12 at α = 0.05. The data arrive: n = 1,600 with 216 conversions, so p̂ = 0.135, z = 1.846 and the two-tailed p-value is 0.0648. The team then reports the result as one-tailed, giving p = 0.0324, and calls the campaign a success.What exactly is wrong with this?
ANothing — a one-tailed test is legitimate when the result went in the expected direction.
BThe z statistic should have been recomputed for the one-tailed test.
CBy choosing the tail after seeing the direction, the team effectively kept both tails available, so its true Type I error rate is 10%, not 5%. The reported p understates the false-alarm risk by a factor of two.
DThe sample of 1,600 is too small to support a one-tailed test.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. By choosing the tail after seeing the direction, the team effectively kept both tails available, so its true Type I error rate is 10%, not 5%. The reported p understates the false-alarm risk by a factor of two.

The validity of a one-tailed test rests entirely on the tail having been fixed before the data were seen. A team that would have used the left tail had the result gone the other way has, in effect, a rejection region covering both tails at 5% each — a genuine α of 0.10. Reporting 0.0324 as though α were 0.05 is therefore a misstatement of the evidence, not a matter of taste. The honest options were to report the two-tailed p = 0.0648 and say the campaign fell short of significance, or to have pre-registered a one-tailed test with a business justification.

4
A supplier's contract specifies a defect rate of no more than 30% of shipments requiring rework. An audit of 500 shipments finds 170 needing rework.Testing H₀: p = 0.30 against H₁: p > 0.30, what is the test statistic?
Az = 0.040
Bz = 43.64
Cz = 1.888
Dz = 1.952
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. z = 1.952

p̂ = 170/500 = 0.34. The standard error for a test is built from the hypothesised value: SE = √[0.30 × 0.70/500] = √0.00042 = 0.020494. Then z = (0.34 − 0.30)/0.020494 = 1.952. Check the condition first: np₀ = 150 and n(1−p₀) = 350, both far above 5. Since 1.952 > 1.645, reject H₀ at α = 0.05 (p = 0.0255).

5
A report states: “The test returned p = 0.03, so there is a 97% probability that our new pricing model increases revenue.”How should this be assessed?
ACorrect only if the test was two-tailed.
BIncorrect. The p-value is computed assuming H₀ is true, so it is P(data | H₀) and cannot be turned into a probability about the hypothesis. It also says nothing about the size of any revenue increase.
CIncorrect, because the probability should be stated as 3%, not 97%.
DCorrect, provided the sample was random.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. Incorrect. The p-value is computed assuming H₀ is true, so it is P(data | H₀) and cannot be turned into a probability about the hypothesis. It also says nothing about the size of any revenue increase.

Two errors are stacked here. The first is inverting the conditional: p = P(data at least this extreme | H₀ true), whereas the report claims something about P(H₁ true | data). Those differ in the same way P(wet | raining) differs from P(raining | wet), and a calculation that begins by assuming H₀ cannot end by reporting the probability that H₀ is false. The second is that H₁ is not a single hypothesis — it covers every value other than the null — so there is no one alternative for a 97% to describe. A Bayesian analysis with a stated prior can produce a probability about a hypothesis, but it is a different calculation and it is not what a p-value is.

6
An analyst runs separate tests of campaign effectiveness on twelve customer segments, each at α = 0.05. One segment returns p = 0.03. The report headlines that segment as a confirmed opportunity.What is the problem, and what is the corrected threshold?
AThe problem is the sample size within each segment, which should have been checked first.
BThe segments should have been tested two-tailed rather than one-tailed.
CWith twelve independent tests the chance of at least one false positive is 1 − 0.95¹² = 0.46, so a single “significant” result is unremarkable. A Bonferroni threshold of 0.05/12 = 0.0042 would be required, and p = 0.03 does not meet it.
DNo problem — p = 0.03 is below 0.05 by any standard.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. With twelve independent tests the chance of at least one false positive is 1 − 0.95¹² = 0.46, so a single “significant” result is unremarkable. A Bonferroni threshold of 0.05/12 = 0.0042 would be required, and p = 0.03 does not meet it.

Running many tests inflates the family-wide error rate. If nothing at all were happening in any of the twelve segments, the probability of at least one result below 0.05 is 1 − 0.95¹² = 0.4596 — close to a coin flip. Finding one significant segment among twelve is therefore roughly what pure noise produces. The Bonferroni correction divides α by the number of tests: 0.05/12 = 0.00417, and the observed 0.03 is seven times too large to clear it. The alternative to correcting is to label the finding exploratory and confirm it in a fresh sample before anyone acts on it.

7
A quality engineer tests whether a filling line's variability has risen above the specified σ = 4.5 g. A sample of 20 units gives s = 5.6 g. The relevant upper critical value at df = 19 and α = 0.05 is 30.144.What is the test statistic and the decision?
Aχ² = 30.973 — reject H₀
Bχ² = 23.644 — fail to reject H₀
Cχ² = 1.549 — fail to reject H₀
Dχ² = 29.424 — fail to reject H₀
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. χ² = 29.424 — fail to reject H₀

χ² = (n − 1)s²/σ₀² = 19 × 5.6² / 4.5² = 19 × 31.36 / 20.25 = 595.84/20.25 = 29.424. Since 29.424 < 30.144, the statistic falls short of the critical value and we fail to reject H₀ (p = 0.0596). This is a genuinely borderline result and the report should say so: at α = 0.10 the same data would reject. The correct conclusion is that the evidence of excess variability is suggestive but not conclusive, and a larger sample is warranted before contractual action.

8
Two studies test the same H₀: μ = 330 seconds. Study A has n = 40 and finds x̄ = 318 (p = 0.092). Study B has n = 250,000 and finds x̄ = 329.7 (p = 0.00086). Both use σ = 45.Which study found the more important result, and why?
AStudy A. Its observed difference is 12 seconds against Study B's 0.3 seconds — forty times larger — and its Cohen's d is 0.267 against 0.007. Study B's significance comes entirely from its sample size, not from the size of the effect.
BStudy B, because its p-value is a thousand times smaller.
CStudy B, because a larger sample is always more reliable.
DNeither — p-values cannot be compared across studies.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. Study A. Its observed difference is 12 seconds against Study B's 0.3 seconds — forty times larger — and its Cohen's d is 0.267 against 0.007. Study B's significance comes entirely from its sample size, not from the size of the effect.

This is the central trap of the chapter. A p-value depends on the effect and on n, so a large enough sample makes any departure from H₀ significant. Study B's 0.3-second difference is real but commercially meaningless: across 250,000 calls it amounts to 75,000 seconds, or about 21 agent-hours a year. Study A's 12-second difference, if genuine, is worth roughly 833 hours on the same volume — but with n = 40 the study lacked the precision to establish it. The correct reading is that Study A found something worth investigating with a larger sample, and Study B found something not worth acting on however certain it is.

9
An operations director wants a pilot able to detect a 5-point improvement in a satisfaction score with 90% power at α = 0.05, one-tailed. The historical standard deviation is 15 points.How many branches must the pilot cover?
A25
B10
C78
D36
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. 78

n = [ (zcrit + zβ) σ / δ ]², where zcrit = zα = 1.645 because the test is one-tailed (a two-tailed design would use zα/2 = 1.960) and zβ = 1.282 for 90% power. So n = [(1.645 + 1.282) × 15 / 5]² = [2.927 × 3]² = (8.781)² = 77.1 → round up to 78. Note that the power calculation belongs at the design stage: once the data are collected, an underpowered study cannot be rescued, and its null result will carry no information.

10
A franchise audit gives a 95% confidence interval for mean food cost of [31.61%, 33.39%] from n = 15. A colleague separately runs a two-tailed t-test of H₀: μ = 33.0 at α = 0.05 and asks what it will show.What can you say without doing the test?
AThe test will reject H₀ only if the sample size is increased.
BNothing — an interval and a test are different procedures and can disagree.
CThe test will reject H₀, because 33.0 is close to the upper bound.
DThe test will fail to reject H₀, because 33.0 lies inside the interval. A 95% interval is exactly the set of null values a two-sided 5% test would not reject.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. The test will fail to reject H₀, because 33.0 lies inside the interval. A 95% interval is exactly the set of null values a two-sided 5% test would not reject.

For a two-sided test, the interval and the test are the same calculation read in opposite directions. The interval is x̄ ± t·SE; the test rejects μ₀ when |x̄ − μ₀| > t·SE — which happens exactly when μ₀ falls outside the interval. Since 33.0 sits inside [31.61, 33.39], the test cannot reject it, and indeed t = −1.200 against a critical ±2.145, p = 0.250. This equivalence is also the cleanest argument against “accepting H₀”: the interval is full of other values the test would equally have failed to reject — 32.0 and 33.2 among them — so failing to reject 33.0 does not single it out.

11
A test of H₀: μ = 50 against H₁: μ ≠ 50 gives t = 2.00 with df = 35. The two-tailed critical value at α = 0.05 is 2.030.What is the correct decision and p-value?
AFail to reject H₀; p = 0.053
BFail to reject H₀; p = 0.027
CReject H₀; p = 0.027
DReject H₀; p = 0.053
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. Fail to reject H₀; p = 0.053

|t| = 2.00 < 2.030, so the statistic falls just short of the rejection region and we fail to reject H₀. The two-tailed p-value is 2 × P(T₃₅ > 2.00) = 2 × 0.0267 = 0.0533, which is consistent: 0.0533 > 0.05. This is a textbook borderline case, and the professional report says “p = 0.053” rather than “not significant” — the difference between 0.053 and 0.047 is not a real distinction in the evidence, and labelling one a discovery and the other a non-result is an artefact of the threshold.

12
A researcher writes: “We tested H₀: μ ≤ 100 against H₁: μ > 100.”What is wrong with the way these hypotheses are stated, and does it matter?
AThe inequality should be reversed: H₀: μ ≥ 100 against H₁: μ < 100.
BH₀ must name a single value in order for a sampling distribution to be built around it, so the testable form is H₀: μ = 100. In practice the test is computed at the boundary and the two forms give identical arithmetic — but the equals sign is what makes the calculation possible.
CNothing is wrong; H₀ may name a range of values, provided H₁ is directional.
DH₁ should have been μ ≥ 100, to cover the boundary case.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. H₀ must name a single value in order for a sampling distribution to be built around it, so the testable form is H₀: μ = 100. In practice the test is computed at the boundary and the two forms give identical arithmetic — but the equals sign is what makes the calculation possible.

A test needs one specific value to centre the sampling distribution on. “μ ≤ 100” names a whole range, and there is no single distribution to compute a p-value from. The resolution is that the test is always evaluated at the boundary, μ = 100, because that is the value in H₀ least favourable to rejection — if the data are surprising under μ = 100, they are even more surprising under any smaller μ. So the two notations produce exactly the same z, the same p and the same decision. Many textbooks do write the compound form, and because the arithmetic is identical it is not an error of substance — but the range as written cannot be computed from, and H₀: μ = 100 makes the mechanism visible. It is the safer habit in an exam.

13
A random sample of 25 invoices has a mean of 18.4 minutes and a sample standard deviation of 2.5 minutes, against a claimed processing standard of 20 minutes. The population standard deviation is unknown.Testing H₀: μ = 20 against H₁: μ ≠ 20, what is the test statistic and the decision at α = 0.05?
At = −3.20, fail to reject
Bt = −0.64, fail to reject
Cz = −3.20, reject
Dt = −3.20, reject
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. t = −3.20, reject

σ is unknown, so this is a t-test with df = 25 − 1 = 24. SE = 2.5/√25 = 2.5/5 = 0.50, and t = (18.4 − 20)/0.50 = −3.20. The two-tailed critical value at df = 24 is ±2.064, and |−3.20| > 2.064, so reject H₀ (p = 0.0038). There is strong evidence that mean processing time differs from — specifically, is below — the claimed 20-minute standard.

14
An A/B test on a website runs for six weeks. The analyst checks the p-value every morning and stops the test the day it first drops below 0.05, reporting a significant improvement.Why is this conclusion unreliable?
AThe p-value fluctuates as data accumulate, so checking repeatedly and stopping at the first crossing gives many chances to cross 0.05 by luck alone. The true Type I error rate is far above 5%.
BSix weeks is too short a period for an A/B test.
CThe analyst should have used a one-tailed test, since only an improvement matters.
DThe p-value cannot be computed until all the data are collected.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. The p-value fluctuates as data accumulate, so checking repeatedly and stopping at the first crossing gives many chances to cross 0.05 by luck alone. The true Type I error rate is far above 5%.

This is optional stopping, a form of p-hacking, and it is the multiple-testing problem in disguise. Each morning's check is effectively another test, so across forty-two days there are forty-two chances for random fluctuation to push the p-value below the threshold — and under a true null it will do so far more often than 5% of the time. Simulation studies routinely find true error rates above 20% for this practice. The defences are to fix the sample size in advance from a power calculation and analyse once, or to use a sequential design with correctly adjusted boundaries that account for the repeated looks.

15
A supplier audit tests H₀: p = 0.04 against H₁: p > 0.04 and rejects, with p̂ = 0.052 and p = 0.003. The supplier replies: “A difference of 1.2 percentage points on 2,000 loans is 24 extra defaults. That is commercially trivial.”How should an analyst respond?
AThe supplier is wrong because p = 0.003 proves the difference is large.
BThe supplier has raised a legitimate and separate question. The test establishes that the excess is real, not that it matters — so the answer requires pricing 1.2 percentage points against the portfolio, which on a large book is substantial, and reporting both findings.
CThe analyst should re-run the test at α = 0.01 to settle the matter.
DThe supplier is right; a 1.2-point difference is within normal variation.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. The supplier has raised a legitimate and separate question. The test establishes that the excess is real, not that it matters — so the answer requires pricing 1.2 percentage points against the portfolio, which on a large book is substantial, and reporting both findings.

Statistical significance and commercial materiality are separate questions, and a complete audit answers both. The test has done its job: an excess of this size is very unlikely to be sampling noise, so the difference is real. Whether it is worth acting on depends on arithmetic the test does not perform — 1.2 percentage points on a ₱4 billion book is roughly ₱48 million of additional exposure, which is not trivial by any reading. The professional response neither dismisses the supplier's point nor answers it with a p-value: it reports the effect size, the confidence interval, and the peso consequence alongside the test.

16
For a two-tailed test at α = 0.01 with a large sample and σ known:What is the critical value, and what would it be for the same test at one tail?
A±2.576 two-tailed; 1.960 one-tailed
B±1.960 two-tailed; 1.645 one-tailed
C±2.576 two-tailed; 2.326 one-tailed
D±2.326 two-tailed; 2.576 one-tailed
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. ±2.576 two-tailed; 2.326 one-tailed

At α = 0.01 two-tailed, 0.005 sits in each tail, giving z = ±2.576. At α = 0.01 one-tailed, the whole 0.01 sits in one tail, giving z = 2.326. The one-tailed value is always the smaller of the two, which is precisely why a one-tailed test is easier to pass and why the tail must be fixed before the data are seen.

17
You are writing the statistical methods paragraph for a study that will test whether a new onboarding flow changes completion time.Which set of elements makes the paragraph professionally complete?
AThe hypotheses with the tail justified; α fixed in advance and any multiplicity correction; the test statistic with its degrees of freedom and the exact p-value; the effect size and a confidence interval in minutes; the sample size and design; and — if the result is null — the power to detect an effect worth detecting.
BThe hypotheses, the p-value, and a statement that the result is statistically significant.
CThe confidence interval alone, since it contains everything the test would tell you.
DThe p-value and the decision at α = 0.05.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: A. The hypotheses with the tail justified; α fixed in advance and any multiplicity correction; the test statistic with its degrees of freedom and the exact p-value; the effect size and a confidence interval in minutes; the sample size and design; and — if the result is null — the power to detect an effect worth detecting.

Each element does a distinct job. The hypotheses and tail must be justified from the business question, or a reader cannot tell whether the tail was chosen after the fact. α fixed in advance and any correction belong in the methods, not the results. The statistic, df and exact p let a reader apply their own threshold rather than yours. The effect size and interval in minutes answer “how much”, which no p-value does. The sample size and design cover the non-sampling errors invisible to every formula in this chapter. And for a null result the power is essential, because without it “no significant difference” cannot be distinguished from “we could not have found one”.

18
A test of H₀: σ = 3.0 g against H₁: σ > 3.0 g on n = 250 packs returns χ² = 341.2 with p = 0.0009. The analyst notes that the net weights are strongly right-skewed but argues: “n = 250 is large, so the Central Limit Theorem makes this valid.”How should the argument be assessed?
ASound, provided a finite population correction is applied.
BUnsound, because the chi-square test requires n ≤ 30.
CUnsound. The CLT applies to sample means, not to sample variances. The chi-square test requires an approximately normal population and is not robust to skew at any sample size.
DSound — n = 250 far exceeds any conventional threshold.
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: C. Unsound. The CLT applies to sample means, not to sample variances. The chi-square test requires an approximately normal population and is not robust to skew at any sample size.

This is the most consequential assumption in the chapter and the one most often waved away. The CLT guarantees that the sampling distribution of x̄ approaches normality as n grows, which is why t-tests for a mean are robust to non-normality. It says nothing about the sampling distribution of s², whose reference distribution is chi-square only when the underlying population is normal. With strongly skewed data this test can be badly wrong at n = 25 and equally wrong at n = 2,500 — more data buys precision around the wrong distribution. The standard modern remedy is a bootstrap procedure, which makes no normality assumption.

19
A one-tailed test at α = 0.05 with σ = 15 and n = 36 aims to detect a 5-point improvement over the null value.What is the power of this test against that specific alternative?
A0.95
B0.64
C0.05
D0.80
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: B. 0.64

SE = 15/√36 = 2.50. The critical value sits at μ₀ + 1.645(2.50) = μ₀ + 4.11. If the true mean is μ₀ + 5, then the distance from the true mean to the critical value is (4.11 − 5)/2.50 = −0.355 standard errors, so power = P(Z > −0.355) = 0.639. In words: even when the improvement is genuinely 5 points, this study would miss it more than a third of the time. Reaching the conventional 0.80 would need n = 56, and 0.90 would need n = 78.

20
Four analysts report the same two-tailed test result of p = 0.048 with n = 30.Which report is defensible?
A“The result is significant at α = 0.05 (p = 0.048), so the difference is proven.”
B“Since p is close to 0.05, we can also claim significance at α = 0.01.”
C“p = 0.048, so 95.2% of future studies would replicate this finding.”
D“At α = 0.05 the result is significant (p = 0.048), though only marginally; the 95% interval runs from just above zero, so the effect could be small. We recommend confirming in a larger sample before acting.”
Show correct answer & explanation

✓ Correct answer: D. “At α = 0.05 the result is significant (p = 0.048), though only marginally; the 95% interval runs from just above zero, so the effect could be small. We recommend confirming in a larger sample before acting.”

The defensible report does four things: it states the level and the exact p rather than a bare label; it flags that 0.048 is marginal and would not survive a stricter threshold; it reports the interval so the reader can see the effect could be near zero; and it recommends a proportionate next step rather than treating a borderline result as settled. Nothing in it overclaims. Note in particular the refusal to say “proven” — a single test at p = 0.048 is weak evidence, and with n = 30 the study's power against a modest effect is likely poor.

🏦 Case: Tuklas Digital Bank — the quarterly evidence review

Background. Tuklas Digital Bank serves 1.4 million customers through an app-only model. Its analytics unit produces a quarterly evidence review that feeds directly into product, credit and operations decisions. Every sample below was drawn competently at random, and every arithmetic step is executed correctly; what you are auditing is the choice of procedure and the reasoning built on top of it.

#StudyData and testWhat the review concluded
AOnboarding redesignn = 40 new customers. Mean completion time x̄ = 7.6 min, s = 1.5 min. Two-tailed t-test of H₀: μ = 8.0 gives t = −1.687, df = 39, p = 0.0997."The redesign had no effect; completion time is 8.0 minutes."
BReferral campaignn = 1,600 targeted users, 216 converted (p̂ = 0.135) against a baseline p₀ = 0.12. A two-tailed test was planned; z = 1.846, two-tailed p = 0.0648.Reported one-tailed as p = 0.0324: "the campaign significantly lifted conversion."

DContact-centre scriptn = 250,000 calls, σ = 45 s known. x̄ = 329.7 s against the 330 s standard. z = −3.333, p = 0.00086."Highly significant improvement. Roll the new script out nationally at a cost of ₱18 million."

Every one of the six conclusions is unsafe, and they fail in six distinct ways — one accepting the null, one choosing a tail after the fact, one using the wrong standard error, one mistaking significance for importance, one ignoring multiplicity, and one reading a null result from a study that could not have found anything. Your job is to identify each failure, correct it where correction is possible, and say what it would have cost the bank.
